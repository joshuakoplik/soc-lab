# Q3 Internal Financial Review & Investor Update Draft

**Owner:** Finance Department / Office of the CFO
**Classification:** Strictly Confidential – Internal Use Only
**Date:** October 14, 2023

**Executive Summary: Revenue Performance**
Riverside Cargo Co. closed Q3 with total gross revenue of $42.8M, representing a 6.2% increase over Q2. This growth was primarily driven by the expansion of our cold-chain logistics corridor between the Port of Savannah and the regional distribution hubs in Georgia and South Carolina. However, net margins were compressed by 140 basis points due to unexpected spikes in diesel surcharges and a temporary shortage of Class A CDL drivers during the August peak.

**Operating Expenses & Capital Expenditure**
OpEx for the quarter totaled $31.2M. The most significant variance was found in Fleet Maintenance, which overran budget by $450k following the failure of three refrigeration units on the RC-400 series trailers. We have since shifted to a preventative maintenance schedule with Vulcan Logistics Partners to mitigate future downtime. 

Capital expenditure remained steady at $3.1M, focusing heavily on the integration of the new FleetTrack AI routing software. Initial data suggests a 4% reduction in empty-mileage across our Midwest lanes, though full ROI is not expected until Q2 of next year.

**Accounts Receivable & Risk Management**
Our Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) has crept up to 41 days, largely due to payment delays from two mid-sized retail accounts. The Finance team is currently auditing the credit limits for these entities to prevent further exposure.

We are also addressing a specific billing discrepancy regarding our priority account, Global Textiles Inc. Our lead auditor, Marcus Thorne (m.thorne@riversidecargo.com | Ext 412), has identified an overpayment of $12,400 on Invoice #88291-B that requires a manual credit memo before the end of the fiscal month to avoid reconciliation errors in the investor report.

**Outlook for Q4**
We project a revenue target of $45M for Q4, contingent upon the successful onboarding of the New England contract. We anticipate a seasonal increase in labor costs due to holiday premiums but expect this to be offset by our new dynamic pricing model for LTL (Less-Than-Truckload) shipments.
